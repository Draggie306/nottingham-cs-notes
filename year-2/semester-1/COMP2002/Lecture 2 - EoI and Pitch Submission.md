##  Mark breakdown
- Motivation (20%)
- Understanding of the brief and context (20%)
- Preliminary Analysis (30%)
- Management (20%)
- Style (10%)

### Motivation
- EoIs should be tailored to each project.
- Work in sub-teams and harness the diversity of the team.
	- e.g. if one person great at 3d code, pair programming to at least 1 other person, and other team members do UI design, prototyping, graphics and art, etc.
- Aim to convey enthusiasm - come across as energetic and interested. *Can do this with a simple Google search.*
- Show ambition as to what can be done in the project, a few extras more than the minimum required (careful of overreaching). 

### Understanding of the brief and context (20%)
If appropriate, compare the wider project context and also existing products and competition - show why our project can be distinguished with better/faster/etc. Investigate the general ecosystem - different stakeholders who may care about the project, not just the sponsor.

### Analysis
propose some initial approach and motivation, with the appropriate language and methods, and potential challenges (misuses of the app/personal data/busy periods - hand-ins, other courseworks - it is not expected that work is done during this window). 

It is more important to be specific than the design being brilliant - “we are going to make a mock-up in Python to simulate parts of the system” vs “make a prototype”


### Management
Show some plan of team management: high-level problem broken down into small parts. 

Think about how it will be managed: all risks and how they will be mitigated against - agile/scrum, key parts of the projet, who will address them.

Risk analysis could include skill gaps (only certain languages), availability (plans to deal with risks), competing workload (factor this in), project specifics (e.g. hardware only on certain days). It should include mitigations (e.g. pair programming) and a fallback plan


### Style
Have things in a logical order - good editorial standards, try to make it look interesting. Have something interesting looking in the slides to break it up (pictures, diagrams, examples, analogies). This 10% is spread across the video, EoI, etc.

## The Video

5-10 minutes: the hard cut-off is 10 minutes. 

- introduction 
- undestanding of the project content
- technical analysis
- proposal approach and relative merits

Tips:
- Consider a pitch video lead 
- Plan and consider a script
- Consider the audience (don’t talk about the software if the person in industry doesn’t care)
- Don’t overload with content - maximum 7 bullet points or so per slide

Content should include
- Some content from the EoI
- Consider introducing a preliminary approach and mock-up
- Brief project management/remarks
- What will be required from the sponsor - data and equipment

There can be one or many presenters - one person who does it all including voiceover, or everyone on camera. The team should ensure everyone has the chance to contribute. 
- Someone in front of the camera, behind the camera, editing, the graphics, the preliminary plan. 


Style and delivery:
- Make it interesting - be energetic, convey enthusiasm
- Can use appropriate humour - but they should be taken seriously
