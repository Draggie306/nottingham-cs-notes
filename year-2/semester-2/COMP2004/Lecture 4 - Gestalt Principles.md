## Gestalt Psychology

Loosely, it means that something is made of many parts, but seems more than the sum of its parts. For example, a single line drawing may include many elements and depictions, visible within it. 

It has 5 principles on how humans see into a meaningful whole: continuity, similarity, proximity, closure and symmetry.

### Continuity
We naturally follow lines in the way they go, and brains prefer to follow where lines go. For example, in a series of lines, we follow the coloured one.

### Similarity
Different shapes in a series of shapes are grouped together by the brain. 

### Proximity
To give different functionality to the same idea, we can distance elements. Sub-groups within a group can be differentiated, but still recognised as part of one overall group. **Things that have relationships should be grouped together** - this group has similar functionality, another group of icons is different.

### Closure
Brains naturally see shapes “autocomplete” when there are none. 

### Symmetry
We try to divide an axis even if there is no symmetry


### Common Fate
Things going together, such as a flock of birds of school of fish. 

### Prägnanz
To complete and fill in the gaps: shapes together 

### Emergence
Collection of blocks. 


## Examples of Gestalt principles

A TV remote:
- Has a peanut-shape, with buttons aligned to the overall shape (of the hand). The key thing is the pause/OK button is central, placed directly under the thumb, given a different colour, highlighting it, etc. Shape and continuity can be used, with a single panel with different but related buttons (rewind/fast-forward). Proximity - numpad at the bottom to join all 

Google search:
- proximity with negative grouping to separate things/results, and to draw the user to specific content. 
- Similarity to group similar things, such as images and results, together. 
- Continuity is used, with all results aligned. There is a flow, with left->right big->small images, aiding scanning behaviour. Within each group, there are sub-groups
- The only thing to see on the Google homepage uses lots of negative space, drawing the user to just the search bar, and symmetry is used to align elements. Good contrast with the logo. There is grouping and sub-grouping, with the “Google Search” and “I’m Feeling Lucky” being smaller, showing that they may not be as useful or as important.
