

Participatory design involves users in the design process. **It is an approach where those affected by a technology actively participate in shaping it.** 

It began by asking: how do we introduce technology into the workspace without displacing people. It is about equality and ensuring the right to information is upheld. PD was introduced as a way for workers saying “I have a right to understand what is being introduced”.

Including users in the design is important because:
- users are the experts in the activities that the system is designed to support
- designers develop a deeper understanding while users gain understandings of the design process
- we may be experts in designing systems but not experts in culture and context
- it can reduce costs by validating/realising assumptions early
- it improves exclusivity by treating edge cases as core signals
- it creates solutions that people actually want to use

### WEIRD
Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich and Democratic (weird) represents 73% of the published work in HCI, but only represents 17% of the global population. 

We are trained by Amazon, Facebook, Tinder etc. to not hear anything. However, PD in Borneo revealed that a digital marketplace to sell goods should have an attached audio file to represent the real sounds from a marketplace. 

## Summary
The core principles are about reducing costs and time, but is truly deeper: it’s really about democracy, power, relations and equality.
