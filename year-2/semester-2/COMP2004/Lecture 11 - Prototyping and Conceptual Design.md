A prototype is a tangible, touchable representation of a design that users can explore and understand the usability of it. In some design fields, a prototype is a scale model.

### What to prototype

**Workflow** and task design - a series of interconnected options, outlining the interactions that can be had.

Form factor - physical dimensions and size of an input/output device. Phones have a different form factor than a tablet, etc. 

System breakdowns and recovery - what happens when the system breaks and how to recover this.

Difficult bits - e.g. specific sections of a checkout system that involve sensitive credit card info. 


## Levels of prototyping

### Low-fi
Is very low functionality, using a medium that can be completely different to the final medium, just about visualisation of a concept - a schematic, pen on paper, to draw something cheaply, quickly and easily for quick feedback.

It allows communication to be fast.

Sketching cna be used to be quick, reliable and informative

### Mid-fi
introducing some functionality (e.g. PowerPoint and Figma) - testing button clicks, highlighting elements, etc.

It enables participatory design and allows user interaction to be a step further to the final prototype. It conveys the overall design to catch mistakes early on. 

### High-fidelity
The step before the final design, to explore the functionality of the prototype.
