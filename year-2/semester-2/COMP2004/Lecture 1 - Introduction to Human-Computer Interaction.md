
Aims of the module:
- Make things that take into account all principles of HCI


There is no point writing great code if:
- You develop systems that no one is able to use
- Your code doesn’t do something people (or you) want doing
- Your code doesn’t fit into people’s lives

It is important how to manage expectations: everything on the computer is a response to the interaction the human is having. 

Human-Computer Interaction is a dicipline concerned with:
- The way humans interact with computers
- The design of computer-based systems to support humans in their goals
- The support for humans with different abilities and backgrounds
- Supporting tasks within and according to different contexts/environments
- Evaluating according to requirements and usability (is the design good enough? if not, is it iterableon?)

The basic features are the functional: it needs to e.g. play a game. Then comes usability: ensuring how the game can be interacted with. The function itself is to make it engaging and fun. 

![](../../../Images/Pasted%20image%2020260129152905.png)

”Usable” interfaces are designed to put the user, not the system, as the central focus. This includes the **time to learn**, the **speed of performance** once learned, the **rate of errors**, users’ **retention over time** (do users remember over time, making it easier the next time), and **user satisfaction** (the hierarchy of needs).


## Learning Outcomes
Appreciating why good HCI design is important:
- Provide you an understanding of the way humans interact with computing systems
- Design systems to support humans in their goals, prioritising their needs, preferences, and limitations.

Prototyping and Evaluation
- Gaining skills in creating low- and mid-tech prototypes,
- Conducting usability and expert evaluations to refine designs.

Cognitive and Social Aspects
- influence of cognitive processes and social interactions
- Accessibility and Inclusivity – Knowledge of guidelines for good design for diverse user groups
- Ensuring accessibility for humans with different abilities and backgrounds,
- Understanding future trajectory of interfaces,
- Impact on society.

Research and Experimental Methods
- Designing experiments and applying qualitative and quantitative research methods
- Collaboration and Transferable Skills
- Working effectively in multidisciplinary teams and communicating design ideas clearly
