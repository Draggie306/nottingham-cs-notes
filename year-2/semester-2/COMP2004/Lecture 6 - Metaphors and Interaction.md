## Interaction types

### Instructing
- Drawing - instructing a pixel.
- Buttons - click and nothing else.

The system is not asking anything back - no dialogue system, just issuing commands.

### Conversing 
Interacting with the system as though it is human, like a conversation. Very little learning required - versus the instructional mechanism where some need to be “experts”.

It can cause difficulties if clarification is needed or something was misunderstood.

### Manipulating
This involves directly manipulating objects - joystrick, touchscreen, characters moving left/right. Get constant feedback - see, listen to what is going on.

### Exploring
Moving through a space - AR (embedding things into the real world) or VR (completely immersed).

### Direct manipulation interfaces 
Using a pointing device to directly manipulate visible objects on the screen with realtime feedback. These are GUIS, 3D interfaces (games, VR).

The user does not have to learn it: they can just go in and use it. Meaningful visual metaphors allow people to understand it. However, it may use more motor skills - using hands more than cognitive effort. 

Disadvantages: there may not be a easy visual metaphor to represent some events. It can also be simpler to issue one command, rather than e.g. having to drag and drop hundreds of documents. It also focuses the user on the more obvious parts of the interface. It may also encourage immediately taking actions, rather than planning to achieve a goal with the wider system. It also requires space for display and a pointing device.
