## Section 3: Details of Level 2 Complaint
> Tell us your complaint
> Please be as clear and succinct as possible and ensure you include relevant evidence in support of the issue(s) raised when completing section 4. If there is more than one issue, please number them clearly.

Ethernet has been permanently disabled in University-owned Halls without notice.

Ethernet refers to wired internet connectivity via data port sockets in each room. Many students used wired connections out of necessity, regardless of the benefits towards stability and speed of the connection. This is because some devices, including ones still being manufactured, do not come with built-in wireless network adapters.

Many complainants were in University-owned Halls last year where ethernet was available. To have it switched off was unexpected and not communicated clearly. It was, and still is, communicated on the guide for what to take to University (https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/studywithus/ugstudy/articles/top-tips-advice/what-should-i-take-to-uni--.html) to take an ethernet cable, so many students had the assumption ethernet connectivity would continue into the current academic year.

Furthermore, students who were forced to move to Melton Hall from Newark Hall were promised the “same facilities”, implying that ethernet provisions would remain identical, as per the email in the evidence.

When students signed their accommodation contracts, the availability of ethernet was clearly listed on the Digital & Technology Services website (https://web.archive.org/web/20250501022757/https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/dts/communications/internet-and-web-services/internet-access-in-halls.aspx) and students find it completely unacceptable that ethernet was taken away without any prior notice.

Wired connections are sometimes required and the University hasn’t provided enough support for these instances. An example of this is with devices that run the Linux Operating System and don’t have built-in wireless connections. The dongles that were supplied by the University 2 months into this issue do not support Linux. Further, Linux is encouraged by some courses (such as Computer Science) given that applications used in some modules are only available on Linux. Attached in the evidence pack is a slideshow (wttm-part_02.pdf) which was shown in a mandatory session during first-year Welcome Week which encourages use of Linux on a native system as an alternative to running a significantly less convenient and more error-prone Virtual Machine.

Occasionally, eduroam may have problems. Last year, ethernet was consistently more stable than internet which meant when eduroam briefly didn’t work, students used ethernet. However, as part of a separate (but now resolved issue), eduroam was completely unusable and unstable for over a month caused by a “misconfiguration”, forcing people to use and pay for mobile data for hotspots, in addition to causing video interviews and Teams meetings to be disconnected - with no reliable alternative which ethernet would be.

Initially, many people contacted the service desk asking for answers which included many of the co-signatories. Initial contact was made in September 2025 and after a “Waiting for Supplier” update in early October 2025, there were no subsequent updates and staff on the IT Support phone line had no information to hand. Students only received the responses / resolution in January 2026.

## Section 5: Requested Resolution
> To resolve my complaint, I would like the following to happen:


The re-integration of wired networks in University Halls, at latest from the start of the 2026-2027 academic year, but ideally before the spring examination period in May 2026.

Should wired networking not be possible, we would like to have a guaranteed equivalent in terms of latency, stability and uptime, including first-class support for alternative operating systems that aren’t Windows. For example, as mentioned, Linux devices and computers that have no WiFi card can only be connected by ethernet or dongle. 

Further, given the services we were explicitly promised were not delivered by the University as described we are seeking compensation of £350 each. We believe that £350 each is the appropriate level of compensation in accordance with the OIA’s guidance. In our view the distress and inconvenience suffered would fall within the OIA’s ‘moderate’ category. This reflects four months of materially reduced connectivity during teaching time, financial out-of-pocket expenses, and demonstrable, day-to-day academic friction and disruption.
