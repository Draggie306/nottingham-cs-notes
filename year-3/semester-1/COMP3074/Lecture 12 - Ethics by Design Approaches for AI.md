
Ethics must be dealt with from the beginning, as opposed to being a reactive process.

Ethics-by-Design refers to the intentional integration of ethical principles into the design and development process. This is very difficult to achieve fully. 

This includes:
- a proactive, not reactive, design
- understands that technologues are value-laden. Systems are not individual: values, principles and the realises of other users should be part of the system.
- provides an approach to responsible innovation. 
- emphasises stakeholder engagement. Systems will be used from outside the culture of the developers. "Technology for me cannot be built without me."

## Core principles of EbD

**It must be human-centred.** Technology should align with people's priorities - with human values.

**Transparency.** How transparent the model itself is, the deployment, how it makes decisions.

**Accountability.** 

**Exclusivity.**


### Value-senstitive design

Value sensitive design has many strengths and weaknesses.

**Strengths**
- Gives a diverse range of stakeholders' opinions into the design process
- Promotes ethical awareness, making designers consider the social impacts of their work
- Allows multiple perspectives to inform the design, leading to more holistic solutions.

**Weaknesses**
- Operationalises difficulties: translating theoretical values into a set of requirements is often difficult.
- Conflicting values amongst stakeholders, leading to compromises that dilute the effectiveness of decisions.

Getting empirical evidence (from stakeholders, potential users) to define values 


### Privacy-by-design and by default


PbD is a proactive framework to operationalise aspects of privacy in the design process. Just like other aspects of VSD:

- proactive not reactive: identify risks and privacy issues before they occur
- Privacy as the default setting: provide maximum privacy protection from the outset, reducing data collection and making sure users are not exposed to privacy risks through default configurations
- user-centric: users have control over their personal data

PbD systems include Apple and Google Pay: they use tokenisation mechanisms instead of raw card details, to ensure sensitive user information remains secure and private.

Strengths
- Improves user trust in the system

Critiques:
- Even though it is a legal requirement, it is very complex to implement and costly: federated learning systems for AI is costly.


### Participatory Design (PD)

This emphasises the involvement of all stakeholders (particularly users) throughout the design process to make sure the developed systems align with all user needs and values.

- Collaboration:
- Empowerment:
- Contextual understanding


Disadvantages include:
- Resource intensive commitment, particularly for small teams - if there are 2/3 people in the team, this could represent a disproportionate time of development
- Potential for groupthink - it is common for 2 or 3 people in a group to take over and "speak for the team" rather than talking to people individually.
- It is very complex to take all information in from all focus groups or user engagement, and then distil this into a design decision and set of system specification.


### Responsible research and innovation (RRI)


- It is a framework designed from researchers in the "global north" and not all considerations may be as easy to implement in other areas of the world.
- Tensions within the interests can be very difficult to resolve.

### Proactive vs reactive ethics

Before ethics by design, systems were designed and were only redesigned when issues arose. We anticipate issues, including unintended consequences. The most important design principle is **malleability**: a system may be used in hundreds of different ways to do many other things that were not intended by the designer.
