
Left unregulated, AI can produce significant and potentially irreversible societal harm. It is built in to daily life, and interacted with across education, economic opportunity and social interaction. Thus it calls for oversight for equitable access and ethical use.

### The regulation dilemma
Regulating too early can cause "regulation chill" - stifling new technology and limiting benefits. Rules written early can become obsolete. Regulating too late results in  harmful practices becoming entrenched, making remediation harder and solidifying power dynamics and inequality.

### Implications for AI system developers

1. Document system design and training data. Design processes, architecture choices and training data for compliance and future audits.
2. Conduct bias and safety assessments. Standardised evaluation protocols across demographic groups can reduce harmful outcomes. 
3. Provide transparency to users. The system should explain how it works, what data it uses and what the outputs mean. Users should be able to change how the system responds, or what data it uses.

The UK governance approach may be significant with a full AI Act, but for the moment it is sector-specific.
