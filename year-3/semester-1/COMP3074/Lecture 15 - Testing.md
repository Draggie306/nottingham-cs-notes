

## User testing
Users much firstly understand that they can talk to the system - this is **discoverability**. A dialogue box on the screen is intuitive and self explanatory.

If someone who designed Alexa without considering that there be a family of many voices, then the system may not be able to handle this.

Recruiting users, collecting data, analysing this data are all important questions in user testing. In UG projects, testing often loses marks: what type of data, how was it analysied, etc.

We test to identify and analyse the real problems and real challenges: products made in the lab are designed to go into the real world. Experiences that designers may not have experienced or made the product for will be tested by real users, so that the overall product can be improved. Depending on the target audience, experts or those with local knowledge can also be used to redeign the system. 

### Purpose
Measuring or testing can be done in terms of user experience (did you like using the system, was it easy to use): is the system usability good for most users? The system should allow the user to perform the real task that the user wanted to do, not what the system decided to. 

Response times can also be analysed, and the accuracy of the information being generated by the system. Boundaries must be set to define the boundaries of the system - "what will the system do, to consider its design successful". Quantitative and qualitative data can be generated and analysed. 

These are all combined to redesign the system - to better fit the realities of the user and the interests. Testing is conducted from the early stage all to the end stag


### User studies

Goal:
- What is the goal and purpose of the system? A specific user task within the system
- Structure and design the research question based on this

Task definition:
- Identifies the parts of the system to test. Could be **frequently used** tasks, or maybe those of **higher risk** - does not have to be all types of the system. It should address the **major goals** of the system, and the design criteria created during the product requirements definition .
- Tasks definitions should be written carefully to avoid biasing the participant or influencing the result. Room should be left to allow users to give real world feedback from the task.
- It can be designed as a **goal description**, such as "order 2 dishes to my house" without mentioning command words or strategies to complete the task.

Task order:
- Order effects are often produced: it is common for users to remember the first and last task. Therefore, tasks should be randomised if possible: allows users to give a range of different answers.

Participants:
- Stakeholders should have a variety of different demographics: race, gender, age, experience.

Data collection
- Must be defined and structured.

Data analysis:
- Quantitative data can be use can be used to test a hypothesis: 
- Qualitative data can be analysed through thematic analysis, conversation analysis, and interaction analysis. These are very structured

Usability testing:
- Usually done when there is a working app.

- It measures both performance (objective) and the experience (subjective) of the system.

### Settings and environments
There is a big difference with in-car/in-home testing and in the lab. Both are important, but the real life situation will encounter real challenges that will not be thought about in the lab and edge cases. **Ecological validity** is a key quality of research studies: how generalisable is the system?

Testing does not end after the initial implemetnation: there are prerelease testing, pilot testing and more. DIffernet tests can be conducted depending on the stage.

In VUIs, there is dialog traversal testing, recognition testing, load testing. In pilot testing, there is success criteria evaluation, logging (making sure every record and data point is captured) and more.
