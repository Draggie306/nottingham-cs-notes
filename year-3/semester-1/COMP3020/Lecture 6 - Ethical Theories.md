

## Where ethical theories fit

Metaethics:
- WHat is mortality itself? Are there moral facts or only opinions?
- Is "harming users is wrong" true in the way 2 + 2 = 4 is?

Normative ethics: 
- What makes an action right or wrong?
- **This is where ethical theories are considered.**

Applied ethics:
- WHat should be done about a specific issue: AI when hiring, social media for children.
- Most computing ethics are here.


## Consequentialism and utilitarianism (Bentham & Mill)

Consequentialism is about when an action is not right or wrong in itself, but it is right or wrong because of its consequences. The results of the action determines if the action is right or wrong.

Utilitarianism adds two things: does this promote wellbeing (happiness) or not. It is not just about ***you***, it adds up the wellbeing an action creates for everyone affected. There are two ways to apply it: act or rule

There are different subsets of consequentialism, such as ethical egoism - focusing on ***your own happinesss***, and others

### Act consequentialism

This asks "which action gives the best result". What action gives the most happiness to most people? 

Example: if the lying does more good than harm, then lying is fine.

### Rule consequentialism
Rule asks "which rule gives the best result if everyone follows it"?

Example: don't lie to users, as a world where people can trust what software tells them, is better overall.
