## ResX Wellbeing

A pastoral and signposting wellbeing service: wellbeing check-ins, referring to other services, support for disabled/estranged backgrounds. Reactive part of ResX, vs RA being proactive.

Some people with little struggles - little things can be helpful with events:
- Be human
- Be yourself - friendly and caring
- Be honest and vulnerable (in 1st year I struggled with X but Y helped because…)
- Explain what has helped you thrive


To understand signs of struggle:
- Withdrawn - not their self (if Monday and acting timid - might just be a bit hungover VS if usually come to events happy but sad - ask what’s going on)
- Self-harm: scarring, risky behaviour, negative to their health. *Not always portrayed through popular media.*
- Signs of drink or drug use - not police, but if they are doing too much, can’t control it  - if something feels wrong.
- Mental health - MHAS
- Trust your gut - if something doesn’t feel right, let them know.

If someone is struggling: call the officers, tell them as much about the situation as possible, student full name, explanation about what 

If need to tell a student about the help:
- might have to pass on to ResX wellbeing, who will be in contact
- “problem shared is a problem halved”, “they are probably experienced, nothing can surprise them, don’t be embarrassed”.
- Solution-focused team who will support to a conclusion. 

RAs are wellbeing’s eyes and ears in halls, BUT we are students so don’t put into dangerous situations

## Active Bystanding

To make sure to call out anything that may make others feel disrespected in a safe way.

- Direct - if comfortable to challenge in an appropriate away “that’s not funny” or “yikes” to let that student know it was inappropriate; call them “in” to a conversation telling why that is not appropriate.
- Distract - if not appropriate/difficult to challenge - say “let’s talk about something else” or to ask if someone wants to go and get a snack to pull the person/affected person away. 
- Delegate - chat to the other RA on the team, describe to the officer and ask them for help

## Staying safe

- Are there to support and signpost peers vs always coming to you. 
- Be aware of the conversations that happen around you — “need to move on”; no matter where people come from.
- If caught in aggression and it is being take out on RA, must raise to your Resident Officers. 

Take action to reduce the risk:
- Every activity, there is a risk assessment to help act safely.
- Think “what is the worst thing that could happen” and “how could this be stopped”


- Clear the area, allow the person to have some room
- Make sure anything that caused the injury is removed or people made aware of it
- Call the Resident Officer
- Make sure that anything that caused the injury is written down and fed back to the Resident Officer for future risk assessments


- Injury - axe throwing vs making tote bags
- Interpersonal conflict
- Damage to equipment, fire alarm
- Social conflict 
- If someone goes off/is ill/have been left to yourself then covering for that person might be much harder if there is another issue 
- Outdoors - risk of weather change, general public interaction
- If run out of things to go out/free food/crowding could cause injuries
- Allergies and food consideration
- Metal health risks - homesickness, inadvertently  

Room 4

If you see a risk: act calmly but quickly. “That’s not right” -> if they continue, then call security. Tell Resident officer and say “just so you’re aware, X happened last night”

If any emergency (situation that poses immediate threat) to life or safety:
- injury/medical incident
- fire
- assault
- destruction of property
- dangerous behaviour
- mental health crisis
- disrupt operations or damage uni reputation
- (if in doubt, ask)
then:
1. ensure own safety
2. gather information to report the emergency
3. escalate immediately to officer or security.

tl;dr contact officer or campus security immediately. It is RA’s job to stay calm and escalate to ask for help.

If something is not an emergency (not immediate danger/no risk to life) - then be empathetic (listen first and be patient) and compassionate

- Panic attack - wellbeing team
- Injury - security team
	- Do not administer first aid

On third-party sites:
- Contact on-site reception staff to report an incident; if no staff, call 999. Then update the Officer on shift

BEST advice: courage and enthusiasm, forget all embarrassment

### Limits and boundaries

If there’s someone who keeps coming and after a while the boundaries are blurring, it is fine to say “this is a workspace”, etc. 


- If parents are calling into the space, say to contact reception/wellbeing team - do not confirm if a student is in that space
- Leave these spaces, it is not advisable to stay with someone all night.
- Give personal number to offer support at any time
- It can be hard to refer someone without knowing the outcome. Some people latch on to ambassadors but taking on too much can affect mental health. Once shift is finished, it is finished. Job is 4 hours there.
- Safety concerns must be escalated even if the student refuses.


- You can form friendships with other students, but there is a line between professional and personal life. If it’s to support for mental health/wellbeing, that is for that team. If it’s because you have similar interests, want to talk etc. it is fine to go and grab a coffee together.
- Be aware of how actions can affect your role
- Don’t use position to pursue personal relationships - no social visits/coffee in someone’s room - instead, come down to the event tonight.
- Treat all students equally
- Don’t encourage students to contact you personally for RA-related issues
- Avoid adding students on social media.

If you have a conflict of interest - when your decisions or actions might be influenced by personal relationships - communicate it with us. Not a all the details, but just that it exists. It will be treated and resolved. Can be things like exes, issue with student, another RA, etc.

If sensitive information is learnt about someone known personally, remain confidential and avoid action on it. Cannot disclose information to parents, police, friends, or other staff, it cannot be disclosed without consent. Instead, explain you may need to share details with your manager or colleagues.

If a parent calls: “can’t confirm if they are here, but if they are, will let them know” even if the student has said it’s okay before. Even “have you seen X” -> “not sure, best to go to reception”

**Everyone, but the student, should be considered as a complete stranger.** A student is never promised full confidentiality, but no information about the student should ever be given out; instead, requests passed on to line manager or Resident Officer.


## Confidentiality

> “To offer the best support, I have to share information with my manager or colleagues so we can follow up with issues so they can be resolved. If you share anything that suggest you are at risk, I am required to share it, but I won’t tell anyone else like friends or coursemates if you don’t want to.”

If someone will not talk without confidentiality, reassure them that our goal is to support them and not to get you in trouble. If they still won’t talk, do not compromise on the confidentiality policy no matter how concerned they are.

Signposting to Samaritans or Nightline with more confidential listening service - but Resident Officer must be informed and aware there is support offered.

	- Claim type: “Standard Casual Hours”

5. Enter hours for each day in the time period.
6. If outside of the normal timesheet: message the Resident Officer after submission saying “worked at XXX park/elsewhere/open day in the morning”
